# Introduction Email — 宮川森林組合 (Miyagawa Forest Cooperative)

**Purpose:** First-touch introduction to Miyagawa Forest Cooperative (宮川森林組合, Odai-cho, Mie), inviting a knowledge-exchange conversation about their native mixed-species reforestation, seedling-production council, and J-Credit carbon registration — as a peer forestry cooperative doing work closely analogous to Mitsue's.

---

## How to approach Miyagawa Forest Cooperative

Unlike more trees (a Tokyo-based NGO connecting urban supporters to forests) or HIGHRESO (a company), Miyagawa Forest Cooperative is a **working forestry cooperative** — the same kind of organization as 御杖村森林組合 (Mitsue Kanko), just further along a very similar path:

- Since a November 2022 agreement with more trees, they've run **native mixed-species planting** (broadleaf alongside conifer, not monoculture cedar/cypress)
- They established the **Odai-cho Seedling Production Council** with residents — producing ~130 native species from locally-sourced seed
- They registered under Japan's **J-VER / J-Credit** carbon-credit system as early as 2008 and continue generating forest-derived credits — a funding stream Mitsue has not yet explored

This is a **cooperative-to-cooperative** contact, not a donor pitch — so the tone should be practitioner-to-practitioner, asking to learn from their experience.

Three rules for the first email:

1. **Japanese only for first contact** — no bilingual needed here; this is domestic forestry-cooperative outreach, not an international company.
2. **Lead with the peer connection**, not the AI data center. Reference the more trees partnership as how you found them, and ask specifically about the seedling council model and J-Credit registration process — both directly actionable knowledge Mitsue could use.
3. **No ask beyond a conversation.** Do not propose a formal partnership, joint venture, or funding request in the first email.

### What to expect

- A small rural forestry cooperative office — best reached by phone during business hours (平日 8:30–17:00 is a safe assumption; confirm on answer)
- If a written introduction is preferred by whoever answers, be ready to send a short fax or mailed letter — email may not be their primary channel
- This is a much smaller, more informal organization than more trees or HIGHRESO — expect a direct, practical conversation rather than a formal reply process

### After the first contact

If they engage, consider — *in this order*:
1. A conversation about how the Odai-cho Seedling Production Council was set up and run (directly relevant to a possible Mitsue equivalent)
2. Their experience registering forest thinning/reforestation work under J-Credit — an additional revenue model worth evaluating alongside FIT-based biomass CHP
3. Whether informal cooperative-to-cooperative knowledge exchange (site visit, shared learnings) makes sense once mutual interest is established
