## 4. Does kiri grow that fast? (checked, with caveats)

Literature says yes in favourable conditions; unverified for Mitsue's climate.

- 5-year-old Paulownia: DBH 21.1 ± 3.3 cm, height 15.0 ± 1.3 m ([Journal of Forest Science, 2022](https://doaj.org/article/cc59a29712ee41fa88dd2994c572cbf4)).
- Fukushima, 7.5-year-old 早生桐: ~16 m tall, girth >70 cm, ~560 kg biomass/tree; CO2 absorption est. 46.8 t-CO2/ha/yr ([Journal of Arid Land Studies](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jals/32/S/32_7/_article)).
- *P. tomentosa* in Fukushima: max current annual increment at 5.4 yr, mean annual increment at 7.3 yr (so ~5 yr is early; 7 yr gives more) ([AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/688799767fd4d06c32a96045)).
- 5-year-old wood showed the best properties for pellet production among 1/3/5-year samples (same source).

**Note (Rob, 2026-10-13): kiri is frost-sensitive and needs good protection against bear and deer** (fencing/guards); ask Yamauchi what protection he uses (see Q3). Bear damage is Rob's note, not checked in the literature.

**Risks for Mitsue (御杖村, Nara highland):** frost/cold winters, steep slope & soil depth, **deer/boar browsing** (village spends ≈¥5.1M/yr on wildlife control), wind breakage. High-yield figures come from managed, flat, fertilised plantations.

**Site requirements (sourced, JP+EN):** see [kiri growing requirements on steep terrain](../kiri_growing_requirements_steep_terrain.md) — light, slope position/aspect, soil depth, drainage, frost, wind, altitude; verdict for steep terrain and an onsite checklist.
