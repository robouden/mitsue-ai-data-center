# Forest Permit, Subsidy & FIT Reference — sources sent by Yamaguchi-san (2026-09-29)

Background: Yamaguchi-san's reply to our thinning-process inquiry (see
`docs/outreach/mitsue_email_yamaguchi_thinning_followup.md`) pointed to three
official sources. Summarized below.

## 1. 伐採届 (felling notification) — 林野庁
https://www.rinya.maff.go.jp/j/keikaku/todokede/batsuzoutodokede.html

- **Filer:** the forest owner, or whoever bought the standing timber. If the
  feller and the reforester are different people, both must file jointly.
- **Documents:** supporting documents have been mandatory since April 2023;
  requirements vary by municipality — confirm with the village office.
- **Timing:** must be filed **90–30 days before** felling starts (confirms
  Yamaguchi's "30 days" note). Completion reports (felling, then reforestation)
  are each due within 30 days of completion.
- **After filing:** the village mayor reviews against the local forest plan,
  can order changes; unpermitted felling risks a stop-work/reforestation order.

## 2. 森林経営計画 (forest management plan) — 林野庁
https://www.rinya.maff.go.jp/j/keikaku/sinrin_keikaku/con_6.html

- 5-year plan filed by owner(s) or an entrusted operator (e.g. the 森林組合
  could file on owners' behalf).
- Three tiers: 林班計画 (≥half a compartment), 区域計画 (≥30ha in a
  designated zone), 属人計画 (≥100ha personally owned).
- Unlocks: income/inheritance tax breaks, forest-improvement subsidies,
  low-interest JFC financing, and a **higher FIT buyback price for the
  resulting timber** (see FIT section below).

## 3. 御杖村 美しい森づくり補助金 — village ordinance
https://lg.joureikun.jp/mitsue_vill/reiki/act/110000854.html

- **Eligible applicants:** prefecture, village, forest owners, forest
  cooperatives, **NPOs**, and anyone named in the village's thinning
  promotion plan — Mitsue-it (NPO) is explicitly eligible.
- **Covers:** reforestation, brush clearing, thinning + timber removal,
  pruning, wildlife-damage infrastructure, forest road work.
- **Rate:** flat 50% of standard cost (Nara Prefecture unit pricing).
- **Process:** reimbursement-based — apply after project completion, village
  inspects, then pays.
- **Condition:** land must stay forest for 5 years post-subsidy or funds are
  repayable.

## FIT premium for 森林経営計画-certified ("未利用材") timber

Japan's FIT wood-biomass categories (FY2025–2026, per pps-net.org, sourced
from METI tariff tables):

| Category | Rate |
|---|---|
| 間伐材等由来の木質バイオマス (未利用材 — thinning-derived, plan-certified) | **¥32/kWh** |
| 一般木質バイオマス・農作物残さ (general wood biomass / crop residue) | **¥24/kWh** |

**Premium: ¥8/kWh** for certified thinning-derived wood over general wood
biomass. Certification/origin proof for the higher tier typically runs
through 森林経営計画 registration or equivalent documented forest-management
records — not automatic just because the wood came from a thinning.

Note: FIT is Japan's feed-in tariff for **all** renewables (solar, wind,
hydro, geothermal, biomass), each with separate rates — these figures are
specifically the wood-biomass categories, not solar.

**Relevance to CHP fuel economics:** Mitsue has no operating biomass power
plant or FIT/FIP registration today — these are the *national* tariff rates
any new generator could apply for. This ¥8/kWh gap is a prospective lever for
the "can we pay landowners/harvesters more" question once a CHP project
exists: if its fuel supply chain can document origin via 森林経営計画
(coop-filed or NPO-assisted), it would qualify for the higher tariff at
FIT/FIP application, widening the margin available to pass back upstream.
Note ≥1000kW plants are FIP-only, not FIT (see [[reference_fit_grid_biomass]]).
Needs a follow-up check on exact origin-certification mechanics
(self-certification vs third-party audit) before relying on this number in
outreach.
