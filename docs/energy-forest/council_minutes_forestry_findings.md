# Village Council Minutes — Forestry & Reforestation Findings

### What the village's own council record already says about the forest problem, before this project existed

---

## 1. Why this document exists

`research/council_minutes/` is a locally-built archive of 100 Mitsue village council-meeting transcripts (H29/2017–R8/2026), extracted and searched for budget/contract keywords. That original scan wasn't built to surface forestry policy discussion — it was built for capital-project tracking. This document is a targeted follow-up pass, reading the actual passages where councilors and the Mayor discuss forest management, to find anything that supports, contradicts, or sharpens this project's forest workforce and reforestation thesis.

**None of the findings below appear in `research/council_minutes/projects.csv`** (that file only tracks single contracts over ¥10M) — they're policy answers and budget-line context buried in longer transcript passages.

---

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 御杖村森林環境整備基金 (Village Forest Environment Improvement Fund)

Established by ordinance **議案第1号**, passed at the **same March 2019 (Heisei 31) council session** as the ECC/グローバル人材育成塾 ordinance (議案第2号) — both created back-to-back at that meeting. The fund banks unused **森林環境譲与税** (national Forest Environment Transfer Tax, a per-year transfer to the village running roughly ¥26M–38M depending on year) for future forest projects, rather than letting unspent allocation lapse.

*Source: `research/council_minutes/text/H3103teireikaikaigiroku.txt`, lines 44, 159, 759–868.*

### 2.2 森林経営管理制度 (Forest Management Stewardship System) — the real bottleneck, stated by the Mayor

National law (森林経営管理法, effective April 2019) lets the village act as intermediary between forest owners who can't/won't manage their land and capable forestry operators — or manage it directly itself, financed by the transfer tax.

Councilor 木村 asked in December 2020 what Mitsue was doing with this. **Mayor Ito's answer is the single most useful passage found**: he expects that if the village runs the owner-intent survey this law calls for, *many* owners will hand management over to the village — but the village doesn't have enough forestry operators or workers to absorb that demand:

> 「本村においてはその受皿となる林業経営体が少なく、また携わる林業従事者も不足しており、森林所有者の意向に対して受皿が追い付いていないのが現状です」
> *("In our village, there are few forestry operators to serve as the receiving body, and forestry workers are also in short supply — the receiving capacity is not keeping up with what forest owners want.")*

His stated response: build up the 森林組合 (forestry cooperative) and workforce first, including using the 地域おこし協力隊 (Regional Revitalization Corps) program to train 自伐型林業 (owner-operator-style forestry) workers, **before** running the full owner-intent survey — because surveying demand they can't yet fill would be counterproductive.

**Why this matters for this project:** this is the village's own stated diagnosis, from 2020, of exactly the constraint the [Forest Workforce Energy Plan](mitsue_forest_workforce_energy_plan.md) is built around — the bottleneck is crew and equipment capacity, not tree availability or owner willingness. This is citable, dated, on-the-record confirmation from the Mayor, independent of this project.

*Source: `research/council_minutes/text/R2-12kaigiroku.txt`, lines 503–545.*

### 2.3 施業放置林整備（マネージャー）事業 (Neglected-Forest Management Project) — the village's current mechanism, and its scale

The village's actual current tool for the above: the **same 村森林組合** (Mitsue Village Forest Association, already treated in this project as the sole forestry operator — see [[project_reforestation_principle]]) is contracted to survey long-neglected, un-thinned forest, confirm the owner's intent, and — where the owner can't manage it — the village pays for thinning itself, financed entirely by the 森林環境譲与税. This thinning runs at an unusually high **40% rate** (vs. normal thinning), specifically so the forest needs no further attention for some years afterward.

As of the January 2024 session: **approximately 2,600 ha of neglected forest (放置林) still exists village-wide.**

**Why this matters:** 2,600 ha is a hard, sourced figure worth checking against this project's own forest-area assumptions (the workforce plan currently cites ~7,051 ha total village forest area from the co-op's own report — 2,600 ha of that being officially classified as neglected is a striking fraction, and a concrete answer to "how much forest actually needs intervention now" from the village's own survey work, not this project's estimate).

*Source: `research/council_minutes/text/R6-1kaigiroku.txt`, lines 793–805.*

### 2.4 地域おこし協力隊 + 自伐型林業, and thinned-wood use at tourism facilities — a 2021 policy statement that anticipates this project

In the Mayor's 2021 policy address: 4 地域おこし協力隊 (Regional Revitalization Corps) members were already training in 自伐型林業 (small-scale owner-operator forestry) at the 森林組合, with plans to recruit more as a succession/workforce strategy. The same passage states an intention to **promote local circular use of thinned wood at tourism facilities** (間伐材等の地域内循環利用促進のため、観光施設等での利用施策を進めます).

**Why this matters:** "use thinned wood at tourism facilities" is, in substance, the heat-cascade idea already developed independently in [[project_onsen_resilience_node]] (biomass CHP heat feeding the onsen and other village facilities) — except this is village policy language from 2021, years before this project existed. Worth citing directly when pitching the onsen/CHP concept, since it shows the village had already articulated the goal, just not the mechanism.

*Source: `research/council_minutes/text/R3-3kaigiroku.txt`, lines 1093–1102.*

### 2.5 Recent clear-cut / replanting oversight gap (Dec 2024)

Councilors raised two cases of private clear-cutting (皆伐) in the same session: 0.51 ha at 神末敷津 (the operator voluntarily planted 510 cherry trees afterward) and a larger concern at 土屋原畑井 about erosion risk with no confirmed replanting plan. The village's own admission: once a 伐採届 (logging notification) is filed, all it can do is **encourage** (促してまいりたい) appropriate replanting — there is **no binding requirement** that clear-cut land be replanted, or replanted with any particular species mix.

**Why this matters:** this is a live, current (Dec 2024) governance gap, on the record, that this project's [[project_reforestation_principle]] — native broadleaf replanting for ecosystem value, not just re-planting sugi for future harvest — is positioned to fill. It also means "who replants and with what" is presently unregulated, which cuts both ways: an opportunity to lead by example, but also no legal lever forcing other operators to follow suit.

*Source: `research/council_minutes/text/kaigirokuR6-12.txt`, lines 279–337.*

### 2.6 Smaller named programs (surfaced but not detailed further)

- **美しい森林基盤整備事業** (Beautiful Forest Infrastructure Development Project) — a prefecture-subsidized line item, ¥4.436M in the FY2025 budget (`kaigirokuR7.txt`, line 1618). No further detail found in the transcripts; would need the source budget document.
- **水源林造林事業** (Water-Source Forest Afforestation Project) — appears as an inter-municipal advocacy/lobbying item ("関係団体と要望活動を実施") rather than a direct village project — the village and partner bodies pushing for budget at a higher level of government (`kaigiroku.txt` line 250, `r7dai2kaiteireikaikaigiroku.txt` line 201). Worth checking who the "関係団体" (partner organizations) are if this becomes relevant to funding outreach.

### 2.7 Reforestation funding vs. thinning funding — an important distinction

Everything in §2.1–2.3 that involves **actual village-spent money** (森林環境整備基金, 施業放置林整備事業) pays for **thinning (間伐)** of existing sugi/hinoki plantations — none of it funds **new planting/reforestation (造林・植林)**.

The only 造林-related funding references found are a different kind of activity: the **全国/近畿北陸 水源林造林協議会** (National / Kinki-Hokuriku Water-Source Forest Afforestation Council). Mayor Ito attends this **as Nara Prefecture's chairman representative**, roughly annually (`kaigiroku.txt` line 250, `r7dai2kaiteireikaikaigiroku.txt` line 201, `3009ka.txt` line 442 — spanning at least 2018–2025). Each time, the report is the same pattern: the assembly reaffirms the importance of afforestation and **resolves to lobby national government for budget** (国へ要望、陳情を強力に行うことを確認) — this is an **inter-municipal advocacy body**, not a funding source, and it does not put money into Mitsue's own budget.

**Why this matters:** there is no village-budget line currently funding new tree-planting anywhere in this archive — only thinning of what's already there. That makes this project's reforestation principle (native broadleaf replanting, not just re-planting sugi) a genuine funding and mechanism gap, not something that risks duplicating an existing village program. It also means the Mayor is *already* personally positioned as a national afforestation-funding advocate — a natural point of alignment if this project seeks national-level afforestation subsidies, since he's already lobbying that exact system for other reasons.

*Source: `research/council_minutes/text/kaigiroku.txt` line 250; `r7dai2kaiteireikaikaigiroku.txt` line 201; `3009ka.txt` line 442.*

## 3. Suggested next steps

- **Cite §2.2 (Mayor's 2020 workforce-bottleneck statement) directly** in funding pitches and the workforce plan — it's independent, dated, on-the-record village confirmation of this project's core thesis, which is more persuasive than the project's own framing.
- **Cross-check the 2,600 ha neglected-forest figure (§2.3)** against the workforce plan's area assumptions — worth a footnote or a revision if it changes the addressable-area math.
- **Use §2.4's "thinned wood at tourism facilities" language** when pitching the onsen/CHP heat-cascade concept to village officials — reframes it as "delivering on a goal you already stated" rather than a new idea.
- If pursuing the reforestation/replanting-oversight angle (§2.5) as a policy argument, confirm whether 2,600 ha (放置林) and clear-cut sites are the same category of land or distinct — they weren't described as connected in the transcripts.
- **Use §2.7 in the Mayor meeting** — see the question below.

## 4. Question for the Mayor meeting

Building on §2.2 (workforce bottleneck), §2.3 (village currently funds thinning only), and §2.7 (no village or advocacy-body funding currently reaches new planting):

> Would the Mayor be interested in the NGO taking the lead — in coordination with the village hall — on **reforestation** specifically (new native-broadleaf planting), as distinct from the thinning work the village already funds through 施業放置林整備事業? Concretely: would he support the village setting up **additional funding for 御杖村森林組合 (Mitsue Village Forest Association)** — new machinery and an expanded workforce — so the co-op can carry out this reforestation work, with the NGO directing species selection and site priority per [[project_reforestation_principle]]?

**Why frame it this way:** it doesn't ask the village to replace or compete with 施業放置林整備事業 — it proposes a second, distinct funding stream for a gap the village currently has no mechanism for (§2.7), operated by the same co-op the village already trusts and contracts (§2.3), with the NGO providing direction rather than asking the village to fund the NGO directly. That mirrors the ordinance-based, co-op-delivered structure the village already uses, rather than proposing something organizationally new.
