## p.4
The question of whether to plant and grow sugi (cedar) or hinoki (cypress) here again. Under the expansion-forestry policy once pushed nationwide, Tenkawa Village too planted sugi and hinoki across almost all its mountain forest, except in areas such as National Park special protection zones where planting is legally restricted. The village benefited considerably from that policy through employment and revenue, but today, as forest owners' management motivation has declined and "abandoned plantation forest" has increased, Tenkawa — which experienced disaster in the great flood of Heisei 23 (2011) — has come to question a sugi/hinoki-only forest policy, from both a disaster-prevention and an environmental standpoint.

## p.7
However fine a plan may be, forest-building does not move forward without people to carry it out locally. Since Reiwa 1 (2019), Tenkawa Village has recruited and hired regional-revitalization corps members for forestry every year. By the end of FY2024 (Reiwa 6), 11 people had been hired, 7 had completed their terms, and 4 of those remain engaged in forestry in Tenkawa. In addition, local peers who also became interested in forestry newly established a limited liability partnership (LLP), putting in place a clear organizational structure for carrying out the work. The village intends to keep expanding this kind of development and organization of stewards village-wide.
