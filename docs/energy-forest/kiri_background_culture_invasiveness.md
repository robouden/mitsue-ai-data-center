# Kiri (Paulownia) — Cultural Value, Supply Decline and Invasiveness

**Purpose:** Background for the SANSEI kiri plan (Yamauchi). Combines claims from a US garden video (transcripts: `misc/This is the empress tree. Paulownia tomentosa.txt` and `... 2.txt`, `... 3.txt`, `... 4.txt`, `... 5.txt`) with checked sources. Reference only; the landowner decides. Primary use in the project is biomass fuel; timber value is possible upside, not a plan.

---

## 1. Cultural value (Japan)

- Kiri wood is an emblem of long life and prosperity: newborn amulets, ceremonial rice bowls, coffins (video); also 桐たんす (chests), koto, geta.
- Light, airtight, moisture-regulating; prized for clothing chests. [Nakagawa Masashichi](https://story.nakagawa-masashichi.jp/106795), [Tansu.com](https://www.tansu.com/kiri/)
- Kiri is also a "特用林産物" supporting cultural-property maintenance. [nittokusin.jp](https://nittokusin.jp/bunkazai_iji/kiri/)

## 2. Supply decline (video claim checked)

| Video claim | Checked finding |
|---|---|
| "Almost extinct in native range" | **Overstated.** Domestic production peaked in 1959 and is now ~2% of peak, attributed to lifestyle change and witches' broom (てんぐ巣病). Trees are not extinct; domestic *supply* collapsed. [nittokusin.jp](https://nittokusin.jp/bunkazai_iji/kiri/) |
| Pathogen | Witches' broom is a **phytoplasma** spread by insects (stink bugs, mirids, leafhoppers); the most destructive Paulownia disease in East Asia (880,000 ha affected in China, 2008). [Review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11123829/) |
| Japan depends on imports | Imports from China since ~1972, later the US. One retailer cites MOF trade stats for ~95% imported (**unverified; check 財務省貿易統計**). Domestic demand reportedly rising, shortfall imported. |
| High log prices | Anecdotal in the video. Aizu kiri was once a "golden tree"; revival efforts use disease-resistant seedlings. [Nikkei](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOCC14D2F0U1A211C2000000/) **No price data found; do not cite figures.** |
| Native of Japan | Genus is generally described as **native to China**; long cultivated in Japan. Avoid "native to Japan" in documents. |

Main domestic regions: Aizu (Fukushima), Akita, Nanbu (Iwate), Gunma, Tsunan (Niigata).

## 3. Invasiveness (US) — caveat for planting

- *P. tomentosa* is listed as invasive by USDA and many US states; introduced 1840; the video notes Connecticut's list.
- Prolific: up to ~2,000 winged seeds per capsule, ~21 million per tree per year; seed spread by wind/water, up to ~3 km in mountains. Invades after fire, harvest and other disturbance. [Bugwood/USFS guide](https://wiki.bugwood.org/Archive:IPSF/Paulownia_tomentosa), [Bugwood](https://wiki.bugwood.org/Paulownia_tomentosa)
- Per-capsule counts vary by source; the video's "tens of thousands" is higher than the USFS figure.
- Packing-material story (seed pods used in China crates, 18th–19th c.) is from the video only; **unverified**.
- Relevance to Mitsue: it is naturalised in Japan, not regulated as invasive here to our knowledge (**not checked**). Still worth a note to SANSEI: harvest before or at seed set, and avoid planting next to riparian or disturbed ground.

## 3b. Second transcript: World Tree sales pitch (treat as promotional)

The second transcript is from a US Paulownia plantation company ("World Tree") answering "is it invasive?". It is a seller's argument, not a neutral source.

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Only *P. tomentosa* is invasive; ≥9 species (some say 23), others (e.g. *elongata*, *fortunei*) are not | Plausible but **unverified**. Florida IFAS has a separate assessment for *P. fortunei* ([IFAS](https://assessment.ifas.ufl.edu/site/assets/files/4354/paulownia_fortunei.pdf)); check its result before repeating. Commercial hybrids are often marketed as sterile; verify per cultivar. |
| 2019 USDA-funded 9-year study: 27.3% survival of unmanaged plantings, so not an invader | **Study found:** Berg, Zarnoch & McNab (USFS), 3 *Paulownia* species, southern Appalachians, [TreeSearch](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/60846) / [J. Forestry Res.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11676-019-01021-9). The 27.3% combined survival is real but attributed to **weather events**, was not a research objective, and measured *planted-tree survival*, not seed spread. It says nothing about invasiveness; do not cite it as evidence. |
| Sun-lover, loses in shade, seeds hard to germinate; company uses tissue culture only | Sun-loving is consistent with the growing requirements doc. Seed difficulty contradicts USFS "prolific seeding"; **do not rely on it**. |
| 1840 US seed catalogue; fossils in Washington/Georgia, wiped out ~40,000 yrs ago | 1840 matches the introduction date above; fossil claim unverified. |
| Kiri flower (五七の桐) is the Japanese PM's seal for ~800 years; Paulownia coffin in China 2,600 yrs ago; phoenix perches on the tree | Cultural colour. The 桐紋 link to Japanese government/imperial use is well known, but cite a Japanese source. Coffin date unverified. |
| Fixes nitrogen, removes heavy metals, nectar-rich for bees, shade for coffee/cacao | **Nitrogen claim doubtful** (Paulownia is not a known nitrogen-fixer); do not repeat. Nectar and phytoremediation plausible, unverified. |

Takeaway for SANSEI: if kiri is planted, choose a known cultivar or species and ask the nursery about seed sterility; do not rely on "non-invasive" marketing.

## 3c. Third transcript: German plantation operator (also a seller)

A plantation company's walkthrough of its oldest German site (5 ha, planted 2011 near Bonn, cool temperate climate) and a new Spanish site (100,000 trees). Hybrid varieties named: Nordmax 21 (flowers), Phoenix 1 (non-flowering, for warmer regions). Seller's account; figures are self-reported.

| Point | Note |
|---|---|
| Best tree at 15 yrs: DBH ~59 cm, height ~15–16 m, pruned trunk ~5 m | Corner tree with the most light, i.e. the **upper end**, not the average. Diameters "vary considerably". |
| Weak growth in the waterlogged dip; "no wet feet" | Consistent with the growing requirements doc (drainage is key). |
| Light-loving; strongest growth at plantation edges | Consistent; avoids shaded Mitsue valleys. |
| Short-rotation agroforestry on farmland, returns to crops after harvest | Different model from Mitsue steep forest land; **not directly transferable**. |
| Leaf litter decomposes fast, soil organic matter rises (monitored soil tests) | Plausible, unverified; no data shown. |
| Partial shade allows grazing under trees; flowers give honey (mid-Apr to end-May) | Possible side income on flat land only. |
| Value pruning in years 2–4 to 5–6 m for clean timber | Labour need; matters for timber, not for biomass. |
| Hybrids "non-invasive"; no spread in 15 yrs at this site | **Anecdotal, one site.** Same caveat as §3b. |

Takeaway: encouraging cool-climate growth rate (best case ~4 cm DBH/yr), but a flat-farmland model. Ask for the cultivar, not just "kiri". Mitsue's cold, steep land remains unproven (see growing requirements doc).

A narrated video: "a tree that does not burn, doesn't rot, stronger than oak, suppressed by a $51B softwood industry". **Least reliable of the four; most technical claims are wrong or unsupported. Do not cite.**

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Kiri crest (桐紋) on imperial seal, ¥500 coin, PM's residence | Broadly true (government/imperial use of 桐紋; the ¥500 coin carries a paulownia design). Cite a Japanese source. |

| Guqin / gayageum soundboards of paulownia | True for the soundboards. "UNESCO heritage" status of the gayageum is unverified. |

| "Doesn't burn", "Class A like concrete and steel" ("ASME 84" should be ASTM E84) | **Unsupported/contradicted.** Kiri wood burns; this project plans to burn it as CHP fuel. Lightness and low resin are real, but fire-proof is not. |
| "Doesn't rot", "stronger than oak", "lighter than water" | Light, yes. **Stronger than oak: false** (see §3f: bending ~42 MPa vs oak ~100). "Doesn't rot": **mixed**: one seller-supplied lab test (one hybrid) reports durability class 1; treat as unverified for outdoor use. |
| 20 ft/yr growth; 10× carbon of hardwoods; "fastest carbon sink on Earth" | Marketing figures, no source. |
| $15–25/board ft; $300–500 lumber per 10-yr tree | Unsourced. Do not use. |
| "Invasive label is industry suppression" | Conspiracy framing. The invasiveness concern for *P. tomentosa* is real (§3). |
| Sterile hybrid "Cotevisa 2" widely tested | Unverified; check with the nursery. |

| Coppice at 3–5 yrs for biomass/firewood; stump resprouts; 8–10 yrs for structural timber | Resprouting is real and relevant to the biomass plan; yields not given. See the Tochigi paper summary. |

Verdict: only the cultural and cultivation basics are usable, and each is already covered by better sources above.

## 3e. Fifth transcript: "Kiri Blocks" (German building-system seller)

Same German operator as §3c. Shows a **construction use of 3-year-old kiri trunks**: 1 m log sections, pre-dried ~6 weeks, sawn into octagonal profiles, dried to ~15% moisture, joined with pine dowels (no glue or metal). Seller's account.

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Fast growth: 20 cm to 1.2 m in 8 weeks; ~10 m height and ~22 cm DBH at 4 yrs; ">6 m in one year" | Plausible. The 4-yr figure is the same order as the Tochigi trial (15.6 cm DBH, 9.7 m at 4 yrs), so it is a useful cross-check. "6 m in a year" is the best case. Both are flat farmland with care. |
| Wood weight ~250 kg/m³ | Matches our density range (209–335 kg/m³). Pine dowels are harder than kiri. |
| Solid-wood utilisation >70% | Seller figure, unverified. |
| Wall modules 25 cm / 15 cm thick; U-value 0.19 with 60 mm Gutex panel; ~40% better insulation than other construction woods | Plausible for low-density wood; unverified. |
| "Fire class REI 90" | This is the fire resistance of a **wall assembly** (EN 13501-2), not a property of the wood. Do not read it as "kiri doesn't burn" (see §3d). |
| Multi-storey capable (upright fibres) | Unverified; low density means low strength, so check any engineering data before citing. |

Relevance to Mitsue: a short-rotation product of small-diameter trunks that is **not** biomass. It could add value to a kiri plan (and a modular-building idea), but there is no evidence of Japanese building-code approval, and Mitsue's steep, cold sites differ from this farmland model. Idea only, not a plan.

## 3f. Seller data: KIRITEC GmbH ([kiritec.eu](https://www.kiritec.eu/))

KIRITEC (Tönisvorst, Germany; part of the listed WeGrow group) is the timber and building-system arm behind the §3c and §3e videos. The brand KiriBloX® sells wall modules (Hornbach online shop for the small 150 line). Its datasheets are the **only measured data in this note**, but they are the seller's own, and testing labs are mostly unnamed. Sources: [technical data sheet, 07/2023](https://www.kiritec.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/TechnischeDatenKIRITECA4_2307.pdf), [KiriBloX building physics, 08/2025](https://www.kiritec.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/KiriBloX_BUILDING-PHYSICS-PROPERTIES_2025.09.pdf).

**Sawn timber (kiln-dried ~13% moisture; hybrid NordMax21 = *P. tomentosa* × *fortunei* was tested for durability):**

| Property | Mean (min–max) | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Density, 12–15% moisture | 268 (218–353) kg/m³ | Oven-dry 253 (205–335); matches our 209–335 range |
| Bending strength / modulus | 42.5 (36–50) MPa / 5,033 MPa | Roughly 40% of oak/spruce strength; stiffness about 40–50% |
| Compression parallel to grain | 23.4 (14.6–40.3) MPa | Wide scatter |
| Tension parallel to grain | 43 (27–62) MPa | |
| Shrinkage radial / tangential | 0.69% / 2.46% | Low; good stability |
| Thermal conductivity | 0.09 W/mK | Good insulator |
| Durability vs. fungi (EN 113-2 / EN 350) | Class "1v" (outer heartwood, one hybrid) | Lab test only, **unusual for such light wood; confirm with the lab report before citing**. Seller-supplied. |
| Weathering test | no cracks/warping after 3 months | Rapid-weathering lamp test (Göttingen), not field data |
| Emissions | AgBB met; formaldehyde 0.004 mg/m³; French A+ | Indoor-air suitability |

**KiriBloX wall system (22.5 cm thick):**
- Fire: REI 90. No test institute or standard is named in the sheet; get the classification report.
- U-value 0.19 W/m²K with 6 cm Gutex board and 20 mm board inside.
- Load tests on one 2.4 × 2.4 m panel: 29.3 kN and 341 kN (two scenarios, unexplained); allowable compression 1,000 kN at 3 m × 1.5 m. No building-approval (abZ) listed.
- Compression (EN 408 basis): fc,0 16.5 MPa, Ec,0 4,587 MPa.

**Marketing claims (kiriblox.de):** up to 6 m growth in year 1, 40 cm trunk after 10 years, up to 35 kg CO₂ per tree per year, "non-invasive" hybrids with documented genetics, regrowth from stump for several harvests, sandy soils usable. All unverified.

**Net:** this corrects two earlier cautions. Kiri timber is **not weak in a relative sense** (decent for its weight) but still well below oak and spruce in absolute strength. The "doesn't rot" line should be rechecked against a primary lab report. The heating value and fuel data still come from our fuel analysis, not from KIRITEC.
