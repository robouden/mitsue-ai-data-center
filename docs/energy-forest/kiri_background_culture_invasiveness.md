# Kiri (Paulownia) — Cultural Value, Supply Decline and Invasiveness

**Purpose:** Background for the SANSEI kiri plan (Yamauchi). Combines claims from a US garden video (transcripts: `misc/This is the empress tree. Paulownia tomentosa.txt` and `... 2.txt`) with checked sources. Reference only; the landowner decides. Primary use in the project is biomass fuel; timber value is possible upside, not a plan.

---

## 1. Cultural value (Japan)

- Kiri wood is an emblem of long life and prosperity: newborn amulets, ceremonial rice bowls, coffins (video); also 桐たんす (chests), koto, geta.
- Light, airtight, moisture-regulating; prized for clothing chests. [Nakagawa Masashichi](https://story.nakagawa-masashichi.jp/106795), [Tansu.com](https://www.tansu.com/kiri/)
- Kiri is also a "特用林産物" supporting cultural-property maintenance. [nittokusin.jp](https://nittokusin.jp/bunkazai_iji/kiri/)

## 2. Supply decline (video claim checked)

| Video claim | Checked finding |
|---|---|
| "Almost extinct in native range" | **Overstated.** Domestic production peaked in 1959 and is now ~2% of peak, attributed to lifestyle change and witches' broom (てんぐ巣病). Trees are not extinct; domestic *supply* collapsed. [nittokusin.jp](https://nittokusin.jp/bunkazai_iji/kiri/) |
| Pathogen | Witches' broom is a **phytoplasma** spread by insects (stink bugs, mirids, leafhoppers); the most destructive Paulownia disease in East Asia (880,000 ha affected in China, 2008). [Review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11123829/) |
| Japan depends on imports | Imports from China since ~1972, later the US. One retailer cites MOF trade stats for ~95% imported (**unverified; check 財務省貿易統計**). Domestic demand reportedly rising, shortfall imported. |
| High log prices | Anecdotal in the video. Aizu kiri was once a "golden tree"; revival efforts use disease-resistant seedlings. [Nikkei](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOCC14D2F0U1A211C2000000/) **No price data found; do not cite figures.** |
| Native of Japan | Genus is generally described as **native to China**; long cultivated in Japan. Avoid "native to Japan" in documents. |

Main domestic regions: Aizu (Fukushima), Akita, Nanbu (Iwate), Gunma, Tsunan (Niigata).

## 3. Invasiveness (US) — caveat for planting

- *P. tomentosa* is listed as invasive by USDA and many US states; introduced 1840; the video notes Connecticut's list.
- Prolific: up to ~2,000 winged seeds per capsule, ~21 million per tree per year; seed spread by wind/water, up to ~3 km in mountains. Invades after fire, harvest and other disturbance. [Bugwood/USFS guide](https://wiki.bugwood.org/Archive:IPSF/Paulownia_tomentosa), [Bugwood](https://wiki.bugwood.org/Paulownia_tomentosa)
- Per-capsule counts vary by source; the video's "tens of thousands" is higher than the USFS figure.
- Packing-material story (seed pods used in China crates, 18th–19th c.) is from the video only; **unverified**.
- Relevance to Mitsue: it is naturalised in Japan, not regulated as invasive here to our knowledge (**not checked**). Still worth a note to SANSEI: harvest before or at seed set, and avoid planting next to riparian or disturbed ground.

## 3b. Second transcript: World Tree sales pitch (treat as promotional)

The second transcript is from a US Paulownia plantation company ("World Tree") answering "is it invasive?". It is a seller's argument, not a neutral source.

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Only *P. tomentosa* is invasive; ≥9 species (some say 23), others (e.g. *elongata*, *fortunei*) are not | Plausible but **unverified**. Florida IFAS has a separate assessment for *P. fortunei* ([IFAS](https://assessment.ifas.ufl.edu/site/assets/files/4354/paulownia_fortunei.pdf)); check its result before repeating. Commercial hybrids are often marketed as sterile; verify per cultivar. |
| 2019 USDA-funded 9-year study: 27.3% survival of unmanaged plantings, so not an invader | **Study found:** Berg, Zarnoch & McNab (USFS), 3 *Paulownia* species, southern Appalachians, [TreeSearch](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/60846) / [J. Forestry Res.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11676-019-01021-9). The 27.3% combined survival is real but attributed to **weather events**, was not a research objective, and measured *planted-tree survival*, not seed spread. It says nothing about invasiveness; do not cite it as evidence. |
| Sun-lover, loses in shade, seeds hard to germinate; company uses tissue culture only | Sun-loving is consistent with the growing requirements doc. Seed difficulty contradicts USFS "prolific seeding"; **do not rely on it**. |
| 1840 US seed catalogue; fossils in Washington/Georgia, wiped out ~40,000 yrs ago | 1840 matches the introduction date above; fossil claim unverified. |
| Kiri flower (五七の桐) is the Japanese PM's seal for ~800 years; Paulownia coffin in China 2,600 yrs ago; phoenix perches on the tree | Cultural colour. The 桐紋 link to Japanese government/imperial use is well known, but cite a Japanese source. Coffin date unverified. |
| Fixes nitrogen, removes heavy metals, nectar-rich for bees, shade for coffee/cacao | **Nitrogen claim doubtful** (Paulownia is not a known nitrogen-fixer); do not repeat. Nectar and phytoremediation plausible, unverified. |

Takeaway for SANSEI: if kiri is planted, choose a known cultivar or species and ask the nursery about seed sterility; do not rely on "non-invasive" marketing.
