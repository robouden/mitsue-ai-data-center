Tenkawa Village in Nara Prefecture partners with the [more trees](https://www.more-trees.org/category/forests/project14/) conservation organization to restore harvested cedar and cypress plantation lands into biodiverse broadleaf forests, notably through corporate-backed initiatives like the "UGG Forest" project. [1, 2, 3, 4] 

## Forest Restoration Project

- **Partner:** [more trees](https://www.more-trees.org/) (cofounded by late musician Ryuichi Sakamoto)
- **Location:** Tenkawa Village, Yoshino District, Nara Prefecture
- **Goal:** Convert single-species needle-leaf artificial timber sites into resilient, diverse broadleaf environments
- **Technique:** Utilizes "Su-植え" (巣植え / triangle cluster planting) to lower deer damage and minimize labor overhead [1, 2, 3, 5, 6] 

## Key Collaborations

- **Corporate Support:** Partnerships with brands like UGG fund continuous broadleaf sapling planting and local maintenance.
- **Local Identity:** Focuses on restoring native tree types suited for the region's headwater ecosystems. [1, 4, 5, 7] 



The **"Su-植え" (巣植え / Su-ue)** cluster planting method is an innovative, low-cost forestry technique designed to establish resilient forests while drastically reducing labor and maintenance overhead. Instead of planting individual saplings evenly spaced across a hillside, this method groups multiple saplings into tightly packed "nests" or clusters. [1, 2, 3] 

In Tenkawa Village, [more trees](https://www.more-trees.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/forweb_mt_annualreport_outlined2021_.pdf) utilizes this method specifically to reintroduce broadleaf species—such as **Kihada** (Amur cork tree)—back into dense cedar and cypress monoculture zones. [2] 

Here is an in-depth breakdown of how the method works and why it is highly effective:

------

## 📐 Mechanics: How the "Nest" is Structured

- **The Triangle Layout:** Foresters plant **three saplings together in a tight triangle** to form a single "nest" (or *su*). [2] 
- **Wide Nest Spacing:** While the three trees inside the nest are crowded close together, each nest is spaced far apart from the neighboring nests across the mountain. [1, 4, 5] 
- **Survival of the Fittest:** As the three saplings grow, they compete for sunlight, forcing them to grow straight and upward. Foresters monitor their growth and eventually thin out two, **leaving the single strongest, healthiest tree** to mature into the permanent canopy. [1, 2, 3, 4]

## 📉 Economic & Labor Benefits

Traditional forestry is incredibly expensive because weeding and clearing undergrowth (called *shitakari*) must be done across the entire mountain plot. "Su-植え" completely changes this dynamic: [1, 3, 6] 

- **Spot Weeding (Tsubo-gari):** Instead of clearing the whole forest floor, workers only need to clear the immediate circle around each nest. This **cuts undergrowth clearing labor and costs by roughly 50%**. [4] 
- **Lower Initial Sapling Costs:** Because large gaps are intentionally left between the nests, fewer total saplings are required per hectare compared to standard high-density planting. [1, 4] 
- **Strategic Pruning & Thinning:** Instead of managing thousands of individual trees, foresters focus their energy exclusively on the designated nests, cutting future maintenance costs to a fraction of traditional methods. [4] 

------

## 🦌 Ecological & Environmental Advantages

Beyond cutting costs, the cluster layout triggers natural ecological defense mechanisms:

- **Natural Coexistence:** The wide, unplanted spaces between the nests allow native shrubs, grasses, and wild broadleaf trees to naturally seed and grow. This creates a highly biodiverse, multi-layered forest floor. [1, 3, 7] 
- **Wildlife Defense:** When three saplings are grown closely together, their dense collective leaves and overlapping stems make it physically harder for deer to easily bite off the main central buds.
- **Stronger Root Ecosystems:** While single-species monocultures have shallow, uniform root systems prone to landslides, the mixed broadleaf spaces fostered by "Su-植え" create deep, anchoring root webs that significantly improve local water retention and soil stability. [3, 5, 8]
