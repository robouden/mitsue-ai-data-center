What is it about this wood that makes it survive what nothing else can? The answer is in the architecture, not human architecture, the trees own. Under an electron microscope, Palonia wood reveals something no commercial timber can match. a honeycomb cellular structure. Thousands of tiny sealed chambers, each one trapping a pocket of air. >> Air does not burn. >> Those chambers are the reason the wood is so light, >> 30% lighter than pine, but they are also the reason fire cannot penetrate it. Each pocket acts as insulation. layer after layer of microscopic vaults holding heat at the surface while the interior stays cool. That is mechanism one. Mechanism two, moisture. Palonia retains a higher internal moisture content than pine, oak or cedar. When fire reaches the surface, that moisture vaporizes outward. It creates a micro barrier of steam between the flame and the wood.
