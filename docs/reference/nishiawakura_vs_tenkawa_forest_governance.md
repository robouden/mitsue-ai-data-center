# Village forestry governance: Mitsue vs Nishiawakura, Tenkawa, Kurotaki, Shimokawa

Purpose: compare village-level forestry governance models against Mitsue's situation (no owner group found; coop as owners' body; independent operators such as Tokuda Rinsan, Yamaguchi; owners choose operators individually). The sortable, filterable version of the main table is the **Village Models** tab in AgentMesh (http://localhost/agentmesh/).

## 2. Nishiawakura in more detail

Flow (from the Future City panel): forest owners (villagers) sign a long-term management-entrustment contract with the village → the village entrusts forest work to the coop → logs go to the local processing company (西粟倉・森の学校) for A/B grade products and to the heat company for C grade → revenue goes back through the special account to owners, the coop and the village. The village also handles FSC group certification, woody-biomass use, recruiting and local-venture support (through エーゼロ).

What it shows:
- One body (the village) negotiates for all owners. There is no coop-versus-private contest for the same job, because the village assigns the work under public rules.
- The village earns income from machine rental, so it has a reason to keep the coop viable.
- Wood is graded and routed in a cascade, which is the same logic as the A/B/C routing in the Mitsue plan.

Caveats: facts are from two brief government summaries. Start year (believed 2008), area under contract, owner numbers and contract length are **not confirmed here**.

## 3. Tenkawa in more detail

Key points (village brochure, 2025/09): tripartite council (village + coop + chamber of commerce), a property district (洞川財産区) that lets one agreement cover a whole area (the more trees agreement, 2021-06-07), and new operators created through the corps (協力隊) and an LLP.

Not found: who executes the work, how contractors are selected, or any public coop-versus-private conflict.

## 4. Kurotaki in more detail

Source: Cabinet Office corporate-donation project sheet (訪れたくなる村づくりプロジェクト). Yoshino forestry's birthplace; mean altitude 455 m. Problem: depopulation and no successors, so forestry workers are declining.

- The village and the coop jointly **recruit forestry workers from outside the area**, with housing and training support.
- Technicians were sent to **Switzerland** for training, and special-felling skills are being built. Result: more requests to manage forests **outside** the village.
- 木工集団 スギイロ (April 2021): 3 coop employees plus a 協力隊 coordinator, passing on 水組木工 and 透かし彫り crafts.
- Funding asked of companies: 企業版ふるさと納税; the village does planning and management.

Not found: who owns and harvests the village forest, coop membership, private operators, machinery, and whether 森林経営管理 is used. The Nara Prefecture Kurotaki PDF returned 403 and was not read.

Relevance: the coop is the workforce engine and the village backs it with housing and training. Closest analogue to Mitsue's coop-centred setting and the Swiss/Nara forester idea.

## 5. Shimokawa in more detail

Sources: town presentation (2015, 環境未来都市), Hokkaido Shimbun coop AGM report, ICLEI member page.

- **Resource base:** the town **bought** national forest (1953: 1,221 ha for ¥88M; 1994-2003: 1,902 ha) to build a base fund and secure jobs. It now holds ~4,628 ha (3,030 ha plantation, 1,598 ha natural) and cuts and replants ~50 ha a year on a 60-year cycle.
- **Who does what:** the town is owner and planner; the forest coop does the work; local sawmills take the logs.
- **Heat:** wood boilers since 2004. Fuel is supplied by an energy-supply cooperative made of **existing fuel dealers**, so the fossil-fuel shops became wood-fuel suppliers instead of losing out. About 60% of public-facility heating is wood.
- **Coop size:** 271 members and ¥480M business volume (FY2024); other sources give 12 to ~64 workers depending on year and definition. Check before citing.

Relevance: shows the "include the incumbents" move (fuel dealers kept in the chain), which matters for Mitsue's coop-versus-independent politics. Town-owned forest is the key difference: Mitsue cannot copy it without land.

## 6. Other Japanese models

- **財産区 / 入会林野:** collectively held forests. After the 1966 law many were converted to 生産森林組合, and many of those later dissolved for lack of income. Inheritance has multiplied registered owners of some commons up to ten-fold, which makes owner-intent surveys hard. ([Forestry Agency](https://www.rinya.maff.go.jp/j/keiei/iriai_rinyatou_seibi.html))
- **森林経営管理制度:** the legal route for a village to pool owners' forests (owner survey → entrustment to the village → re-delegation to registered operators).
- Not found: any compulsory forest body like a Dutch water board (irrigation 土地改良区 is the closest analogue; not researched).
- Candidates not yet read: Yusuhara (Kochi), Kitayama (Wakayama), Shimokitayama (Nara; municipality-led private-forest work, 300 ha target).

## 7. What it means for Mitsue

- **Nishiawakura** is the nearest match to the polder idea (village as contractor, coop as executor, special account, machine rental), and its machine-rental feature fits the plan to share a harvester. It needs the village hall to lead, and in Mitsue the village is both the main lever and the main political risk.
- **Kurotaki** shows a coop-plus-village workforce model in the same prefecture, with Swiss training as a hook.
- **Shimokawa** shows the incumbents-in-the-chain approach for fuel supply, and long-run town-owned forest.
- Practical first step: use the 森林経営管理 owner survey data (ask Ohba-san) and the coop's member list to see how many owners are reachable through existing bodies.

## 8. Open questions

Mitsue: any 財産区 or 入会 land? Coop member count and forest share? Nishiawakura: start year, area, owner count, contract length, revenue split. Tenkawa: how village-forest work is assigned. Kurotaki: who harvests village forest, private operators. Shimokawa: how coop and private contractors split town-forest work.
