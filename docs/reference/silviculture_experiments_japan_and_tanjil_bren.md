# Silvicultural Experiments: Tanjil Bren (Australia) and Comparable Work in Japan

Literature notes for the staged-harvest and native-broadleaf replanting plan. Background only: no costs, machinery or sugi data in the Australian paper. Items marked *(summary only)* were seen via search summaries, not read in full.

## 1. Tanjil Bren Silvicultural Systems Project (Australia)

Hammond, Nitschke, Trouvé, Baker. "Alternative Silvicultural Systems Maintain Multiple Values in Tall Eucalypt Forest." *Forest Science*, 1 July 2026. Open access (CC-BY 4.0). https://doi.org/10.1007/s44391-026-00076-6

- Mountain ash (*Eucalyptus regnans*), Victoria; ~1900 mm rain, slopes 0–40°; stands ~50 years old at harvest (1988–1990), re-measured 2019 (30 years on).
- 7 treatments plus control: clearfell (6–8 ha), seed tree (5% retained), retention 30% and 50%, gaps 0.25 / 0.5 / 2 ha. 53 plots of 20×20 m. Retention = only two 5 ha coupes each (thin replication); single site.
- Largest 50 trees/ha: ~108 cm DBH in retention and control, about twice the other treatments (35–62 cm).
- Total aboveground biomass: retention 552–662 Mg/ha vs control 654; other treatments 105–264. Retention regained all lost basal area within 30 years.
- Gaps (0.25–2 ha) too small for the light-demanding eucalypt: dominated by shade-tolerant *Acacia* / *Pomaderris*, low basal area.
- Hollow-bearing trees: <1/ha in small and medium gaps; 7–11/ha in retention and control (>6× clearfell/seed tree).
- No significant differences in species richness or composition after 30 years.
- Burning vs bulldozer site prep: early difference gone by year 8; mechanical scarification an acceptable substitute.
- Authors recommend a mosaic of treatments across the landscape; retention in 40–60-year-old stands.

## 2. Comparable work in Japan

### Tamura 2014 (read in full): selection logging + deer fence, 12 years
Tamura A. 2014. J Jpn For Soc 96:333–341 (Kanagawa Prefecture). https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjfs/96/6/96_333/_pdf

- 90-year-old sugi/hinoki plantation, high sika deer density. Selection logging on 3 plots by floor-vegetation cover (low ~1%, medium ~30%, high ~80%), each with a deer fence; inside vs outside fence compared for 12 years.
- Saplings >1 m tall at year 12 inside fence: low-cover 11,873/ha, medium 7,499/ha, high 2,083/ha. Less floor vegetation before cutting = more regeneration.
- Outside the fence: no difference among plots, and far fewer species and individuals than inside.
- Conclusion: overstorey density control alone is not enough; shrub clearing / floor-vegetation removal and exclusion of deer browsing are also needed.

### REFRESH, Hokkaido *(summary only)*
Retention Experiment for Plantation Forestry in Sorachi, started 2013, Sakhalin fir plantations. Six treatments (clearcut; dispersed retention 10 / 50 / 100 trees/ha; aggregated retention with one 0.36 ha patch; gap cutting) plus unharvested plantation and natural forest controls, 3 replicates each. About 12 years old, so no 30-year results.
- Design: https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/bes2.2044
- Early findings synthesis: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S037811272400241X
- Harvest productivity and cost (retention <50 trees/ha minimised economic loss; >50 trees/ha cost slightly more, ~9% in volume): https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4907/14/2/324
- Related watershed study: retaining 45–50% of trees kept stream nitrate from rising.

### Row-thinning (列状伐採) in old sugi, 15 years later *(summary only)*
https://agriknowledge.affrc.go.jp/RN/2010812021.pdf — effect on broadleaf natural regeneration; broadleaf seedlings tend to establish where relative light is about 20–30% or more.

## 3. Takeaways for Mitsue

- No Japanese study found matching Tanjil Bren (full treatment range, one site, 30 years). REFRESH is the closest design but is young and in fir.
- Deer control looks more decisive than thinning method for broadleaf regeneration (Tamura 2014). Fits the ~¥5.1M/yr 御杖村 wildlife budget; "wildlife forage" planting needs protection to succeed.
- Partial retention / row-thinning is the transferable idea; light and species ecology differ between eucalypt and sugi/native broadleaf.
- No Nara-specific trial results found. Nara prefecture research list (https://www.pref.nara.jp/56558.htm) could not be opened (expired certificate); the Nara Forest Technology Center could be asked directly.
