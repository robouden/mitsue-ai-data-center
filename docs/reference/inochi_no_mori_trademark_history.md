## Key correction to the prior narrative

Previous assumption (now revised): that いのちの森 was first trademarked in 1999 as a general
forest/environmental concept, and only later repurposed as a cemetery-service trademark once the
森のお墓 project developed.

**What the J-PlatPat record actually shows:** the cemetery-service connection was present from the
original 1998 application — Class 42, 墓地の提供, from day one. There was no "general forest
concept first, cemetery angle later" — the trademark was conceived as cemetery-related from the
start, **13 years before** the いのちの森 burial service formally launched (2016-02-26, per
[[project_mokurai_genmyo_kagoshima_temple]]).

## Applicant name — a caution

The record names the applicant as **宗教法人自然宗佛國寺** — but the temple's own history says the
organization was called plainly **佛國寺** until it renamed to **自然宗佛國寺 in 2000**, two years
*after* this 1998 filing. Two possibilities, unresolved:

1. J-PlatPat has retroactively normalized the applicant's name to its current form in this record, or
2. the religious corporation was already legally using the 自然宗佛國寺 name in 1998, ahead of what
   the temple's own public history states.

Don't state "自然宗佛國寺 applied for the trademark in 1998" as settled fact until this is checked.

## Applicant address — another open thread

The 1998 filing address is **Komono-chō, Mie** — not Ōdai-chō, where the temple's current 寺務所
and the いのちの森 forest land are. Komono-chō doesn't appear anywhere else in prior research on
this temple. Worth asking Myōen/Mokurai directly what the Komono-chō connection was (an earlier
base before Ōdai-chō? A separate registered address only?).

## What was actually protected

Not "いのちの森" as a general phrase — specifically a **standard-character mark** for **Class 42,
墓地の提供 (provision of cemetery services)**. This grounds the later project name
森林自然葬・いのちの森 directly in trademark law, not just branding — the current project (per
morino-ohaka.jimdofree.com) still cites registration No. 4310763.

## Why this matters

The trademark predates the present Ōsugi Valley forest-burial operation by **~18 years** (filed
1998, forest parcels acquired 2007/2014, burial service launched 2016) and by **~28 years** to
today (2026). This suggests the forest+death+return-to-nature idea was part of Mokurai's vision
very early — arguably before or during the 1999–2004 Nara water-source-village reconstruction
attempt noted elsewhere — rather than a later addition once the Ōsugi Valley land was in hand.
