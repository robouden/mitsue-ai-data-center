# Shaku Myōen (釈妙円) — Biography Notes

Background research on 自然宗佛國寺's 薬師庵主/住持職 and Rob's actual email contact (bukokuji@gmail.com),
ahead of the October 2026 visit. See [[project_shaku_myoen_biography]] (memory),
[[project_mokurai_genmyo_kagoshima_temple]] (outreach history), [[project_bukkokuji_visit_idea]]
(visit planning), and [mokurai_biography.md](mokurai_biography.md) (the temple's founder). Compiled
from the temple's own site (bukokuji.jimdofree.com biography pages) plus independent library/book
catalog records (CiNii, NDL Search, Rakuten Books, Tokyo Metropolitan Library) for her pre-ordination
publications. The biographical narrative itself is secondary/self-reported; the book-catalog records
are independent corroboration that she was a real published author under her prior name, not proof
of the narrative's interpretation.

## Identity

- **Birth/pre-ordination name: 高橋由美子 (Takahashi Yumiko).** Explicitly **not** the same person
  as the pop-idol actress/singer of the same name (b. 1974) — this Takahashi Yumiko was already a
  university-trained health researcher and published author decades earlier; a different, unrelated
  person.
- **Dharma name: 妙円 (Myōen)**, received on ordination, 1992-10, Mt. Hiei (比叡山).
- **Current titles:** 薬師庵主 (head of Yakushi-an), 宗教法人自然宗佛國寺 住持職 (head priest, since
  2014), 「いのちの学校」校長 (principal, "School of Life").

## The turning point that led to Buddhism

Per her own account, once her Mont Blanc injuries had improved, she began asking:

> 人は病院で死ぬことが、自然なのだろうか？
> ("Is it really natural for people to die in hospitals?")
>
> 生まれることも、死ぬことも、ごく自然なことで、病院で取り扱うことではないのでは？
> ("Aren't birth and death simply natural — not things to be handled by hospitals?")

This led to roughly five years researching longevity communities — **Vilcabamba, Ecuador** and
**棡原村 (Yuzurihara), Yamanashi** — including hair-mineral analysis, with findings presented to
medical academic societies. Her key finding: interviewees consistently described their happiest
moments as *giving* to others, not receiving — which she connects to the Buddhist concept of
**利他 (altruism, living for others' benefit)**. This became the philosophical bridge from her
health career into Buddhism, and later into いのちの森 (forest burial as a vehicle for ordinary
people to practice 利他 by contributing to forest regeneration).

## Relationship to いのちの森 and Mokurai

She did not originate いのちの森 independently. The apparent lineage is: **Mokurai (founder;
religious corporation registered 1985) → 自然宗佛國寺 → Myōen (as 薬師庵主, then 住持職 from 2014)
→ current operational lead of 森林自然葬・いのちの森.** See [mokurai_biography.md](mokurai_biography.md)
and [inochi_no_mori_trademark_history.md](inochi_no_mori_trademark_history.md) for the trademark's
own history — filed 1998, sixteen years before she became 住持職, which argues against her having
originated the specific name, though her personal water/forest interest (Aso, 1987) predates even
that 1998 filing, so influence in either direction remains plausible and unconfirmed.

   it at least plausible she had more fluency at some point. Not necessarily a contradiction —
   career-specific language fluency fades without use.
4. Exact date of the Mont Blanc accident — only "age 24" is given, no year.

## Why this matters for the visit

She has an unusually rich pre-Buddhist career — published health author, TV commentator, ran
multiple companies — rather than being simply "the person who writes emails for Mokurai." Her own
biography is arguably as interesting as his, and gives good rapport material: her Aso water-source
period and the いのちの森 philosophy share an obvious throughline worth asking about directly at
the first meeting.
