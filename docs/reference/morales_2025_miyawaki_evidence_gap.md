# Reference — Morales et al. (2025), "Tiny forests, huge claims: The evidence gap behind the Miyawaki method"

## Citation

Morales, N.S., Fernández, I.C., Durán, L., Craven, D. (2025/2026). *Journal of Applied Ecology* 63: e70242. DOI: [10.1111/1365-2664.70242](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2664.70242). Open access (CC).
Local PDF: `docs/energy-forest/misc/Journal of Applied Ecology - 2025 - Morales - Tiny forests huge claims The evidence gap behind the Miyawaki method for.pdf`

## Method

Systematic review (ROSES protocol): 244 records → 51 documents. Only 21 (41%) had quantitative data; 7 had a control, 3 had replication. Japanese-language sources barely searched (1 non-English document), so Japanese evidence may be under-counted.

## Findings

| Claim | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Rapid growth (~10×) | Partial — higher early growth; soil preparation effect not separated |
| Biodiversity | Weak — 1 study, no comparison |
| Carbon sequestration | Weak — early stocks higher, no long-term difference |
| Maturity in 20–30 yr | Null |
| Self-sustaining after 3 yr | Null |
| Cost efficient | Null — literature suggests *more* expensive |
| Higher density long-term | Null |

- Cost: ~$12.8k–32k/ha in Asia vs ~$210/ha for the Popham method.
- Mortality in dense planting 33–50%; survival 62–100%.
- Claims originate mostly in grey (non-peer-reviewed) literature.

## Relevance to Mitsue

- IGES-JISE figures (95% survival, 10 m in ~12 yr) are the proponent's own claims; do not cite as established in grant applications (AEON Round 37) or outreach.
- Supports the native-broadleaf, local-provenance, NGO-chosen-species approach (see reforestation principle) over a branded dense-planting method.
- Cost per ha matters for clear-fell-and-restore economics; budget conservatively.
