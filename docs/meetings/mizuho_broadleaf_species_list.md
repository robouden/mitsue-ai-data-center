# Mitsue Village — Native Broadleaf Species List

**Prepared for:** 上坂奈穂様 (Nao Uesaka), Mizuho Securities — Sustainability Promotion Dept.
**Purpose:** Reference list of native broadleaf ("satoyama") species targeted for reforestation as aging sugi (cedar) and hinoki (cypress) plantations are thinned, in place of replanting more conifer monoculture.

---

## Why these species

Selection favors trees that (a) are native to Mitsue's satoyama and ridge habitats, (b) provide seed/mast forage that keeps deer, boar, and bear in the forest rather than farmland, and (c) include at least one species — Kihada — with an existing local/regional market (medicinal bark, furniture) so the forest has an economic path alongside its ecological one. Final species mix and site allocation is expected to be a joint decision with more trees and the local forest cooperative (地域林業パートナー).

## Candidate additions — sacred-grove (shrine forest) species

Species named in Omura (2004), *Trees, Forests and Religion in Japan*, as historically tied to Shinto/Buddhist forests of warm-temperate SW Japan (full note: `docs/reference/omura_2004_trees_forests_religion_japan.md`). **Literature-based only: not yet field-verified for Mitsue's elevation, cold tolerance or local presence.** Check against the field survey before adding to any planting plan.

| English | 日本語 | Latin name | Role (per Omura 2004) | Check before use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sakaki | サカキ | *Cleyera japonica* | Evergreen pioneer; twigs offered at shrines; marks holy boundaries | Small tree; confirm cold hardiness |
| Ogatama tree | オガタマノキ | *Magnolia compressa* (syn. *Michelia compressa*) | Planted at shrine gates ("inviting the god's soul") | Warm-climate species; confirm Mitsue is not too cold |
| Nagi | ナギ | *Nageia nagi* (Omura: *Podocarpus nagi*) | Pure stand in the protected Mikasayama forest at Kasuga-taisha, Nara | A broad-leaved conifer; confirm it grows at Mitsue's altitude |
| Evergreen oaks (Cyclobalanopsis group) | カシ類 | *Quercus* subg. *Cyclobalanopsis* | Dominant in shrine forests; acorns were a staple food in SW Japan | Omura names no species; pick local ones (e.g. from the field survey) |
| Japanese star anise | シキミ | *Illicium religiosum* | Twigs offered to gods and the dead | **Toxic fruit and seeds**: avoid near children and livestock |
| Camphor tree | クスノキ | *Cinnamomum camphora* | Large sacred trees at shrines and temples | Warm lowland species; likely marginal at Mitsue's altitude |

Omura also lists pine (*Pinus densiflora*, *P. thunbergii*), bamboo, Japanese cedar and zelkova as religious trees. Zelkova is already on the list above. Pine is a conifer pioneer and bamboo is not a reforestation target, so they are left out here.

## Planting method

Species reintroduction follows the **su-植え (巣植え, "nest planting")** cluster method used by more trees at their Tenkawa Village project: three saplings planted in a tight triangle ("nest"), nests spaced widely apart across the site. This halves undergrowth-clearing labor, reduces total saplings needed per hectare, and lets native shrubs and grasses fill the gaps between nests — while the close spacing of each nest makes it harder for deer to strip the central buds.

## Status

Species selection and site allocation are not yet finalized — this list reflects the native-species checklist compiled for our own field survey (Mitsue Mother Tree Project) and conversations with more trees, not a signed planting plan.
