# CHP Fuel Supply — Background Notes for the Forest Association's Own Discussion

> **"The forest our ancestors planted — the power that sustains the village they built"**
> 先人が植えた森が、今、村を支える力になる。

**Status: everything below is a draft, and we are still finding a good way through this
together — nothing here is decided, priced, or offered.** This is not a term sheet from us to
the co-op. It exists only to put some market numbers on the table so 御杖村森林組合 — and
Kinjo-san specifically — have something concrete to react to, question, or throw out entirely,
ahead of the open item already flagged in `mitsue_kanko_collaboration_diagrams.html`
§"Discussion points." Every figure is illustrative, unconfirmed, and ours to revise once the
co-op tells us what actually works for them.

---

## 0. Who leads what — the co-op is in the middle of this, not us

This project only works if the **Forest Association leads the forestry side**: how much gets
harvested, at what pace, with what crew, and on what terms. That is their forest, their
expertise, and their call. Our side (the mission entity / NGO, and the KK operating company it
sets up and controls) is not trying to direct the co-op's work — our job is narrower:

- **Build and run the CHP + data center** so there is a reliable buyer for wood that currently
  has none.
- **Set up and operate the KK** to handle the financing, contracts, and revenue administration —
  the parts that are our expertise, not the co-op's.
- **Bring whatever market information we find** (prices, grades, benchmarks) to the table as
  input to the co-op's own decision, not as a price we're imposing.

Concretely, in the existing governance/labor diagrams (`mitsue_kanko_collaboration_diagrams.html`
§§1, 3): the co-op plants, tends, harvests, chips, and dries wood — that whole chain is the
co-op's operation. The KK's role is to buy what the co-op is willing to sell, on terms the co-op
is willing to accept, and turn that into CHP fuel and DC power. If any of what follows reads like
us setting the direction, that's a drafting problem to fix, not the intended relationship.

## 1. Some market context, for the co-op's own reference

Not an argument for a price — just numbers that might be useful background for however the
co-op wants to think about this.

Current sugi stumpage is **¥4,127/m³ (March 2024), down from a ¥22,707/m³ peak in 1980, still
falling** [林野庁 山元立木価格統計]. That price is for **A-grade sawlog** — straight,
16–36cm timber the sawmill wants. Most of what a **thinning** operation actually produces is not
that grade. Per a Japanese thinning-site study (larch, Iwate Pref. Forestry Technology Center,
研究成果速報 No.308 — see `../energy-forest/workforce/mitsue_forest_workforce_energy_plan.md`
§4a for full caveats — **this is not a Mitsue or sugi/hinoki study, treat as a rough reference
only, and the co-op's own experience of what their thinnings actually yield should override it**):

| Grade | Share of thinned volume (illustrative) | Existing market today |
|---|---|---|
| A材 (sawlog) | ~22% | Yes — sawmill, at the stumpage price above |
| B材 (plywood-grade) | ~54% | Sometimes — depends on a plywood/pulp buyer existing |
| C材 (fuel-grade, crooked) | ~19% | Often none, as far as we know |
| D材 (tops/branches) | ~5% | Often none, as far as we know |

If this rough pattern holds for Mitsue's own stands — which only the co-op can actually confirm
— it would suggest that a meaningful share of thinned wood (C+D, maybe more) doesn't have an
existing buyer today, which is presumably part of why thinning has been hard to sustain. If the
co-op's own experience says otherwise, that overrides everything in this table.

## 2. What the KK could put on the table, for the co-op to shape

Not an offer — a sketch of the *tools* the KK side could bring, for the co-op to push back on,
reprice, or restructure however makes sense to them.

### 2a. A guaranteed, multi-year volume commitment
Something the open timber market can't offer: a standing buyer, over a term the co-op sets, at
a volume that follows the co-op's own pace — not a target imposed from our side. If a
multi-year commitment (illustratively 5–10 years) would actually help the co-op plan crew growth
(`mitsue_forest_workforce_energy_plan.md` §1), we'd want to shape it around their schedule, not
the other way around.

### 2b. A delivery rate — starting point only, co-op's to counter
The Mishima-derived benchmark used so far to calibrate the CHP model is a **≤¥7,000/m³ delivered
chip cost** ceiling (`../meetings/forest_group_discussion_topics_jp.md`) — covering harvest +
chip + dry + haul combined. Depending on who ends up handling which step, a starting number for
discussion might land somewhere in the **¥3,000–5,000/m³** range for the co-op's own
harvest-and-deliver work — but this is a number to react to, not a number we're proposing to
hold to. If the co-op's real costs run higher, that's exactly the kind of thing this
conversation needs to surface.

### 2c. A J-Credit share, as a separate strand
Current market ~¥3,000–6,000/t CO₂ (`forest_group_discussion_topics_jp.md` §credits), tied to the
restoration side rather than the fuel price — one more thing to work out together, not a fixed
add-on.

**What this isn't:** any claim on A-grade sawlog. That material should keep going wherever the
co-op already sells it (or however they'd prefer to handle it) — the "Forestry products (timber,
lumber)" line already in `mitsue_revenue_model.md` assumes exactly this, kept separate from CHP
fuel.

## 4. Questions we don't have answers to — for the co-op to decide, not us

1. Does the co-op currently have a buyer for B-grade wood, or does it go unsold? (Changes the
   picture in §3 substantially — and only the co-op knows the real answer.)
2. What does the co-op's own harvest/chip/dry/haul cost actually look like per m³ today? Is
   ¥3,000–5,000/m³ anywhere close, or off?
3. Does the co-op's current crew support any near-term volume, or does hiring/mechanization need
   to come first — and on what timeline that works for them?
4. What contract term, volume ramp, and price structure would the co-op actually want — not what
   would suit us?
5. Species/replanting say — separate topic, tracked in `forest_group_discussion_topics_jp.md`,
   same principle: the co-op and NGO work this out together, not us deciding for them.
