## 2. A very short history of Japanese forests (~5 min)

- Edo → Meiji → now, fast version: who owned what, broadleaf for villagers,
  sugi/hinoki for construction + government. (Pull specifics from
  `docs/energy-forest/history/hundred_year_plan_transcription.md`.)
- The "100-year plan": planted en masse ~75 years ago after WWII for construction
  timber. We're 75 years into a 100-year story — 25 years left before the trees
  are "done" *and*, funny enough, before new power tech (compute, maybe fusion)
  changes the whole energy picture too. Two clocks ticking at once.
- Price collapse joke angle: "we planted a retirement fund, then the 1970s–80s
  cheap-import wave crashed the market before the trees even grew up." A-grade
  sugi ¥12,000 → ¥4,475/m³ by 2025. Only A-grade sells today — that's the punchline
  problem the rest of the talk fixes.

## 4. The problems, plainly stated (~5 min)

1. **Labor** — the cooperative is small and young; not enough hands or machines
   to harvest at scale.
2. **A ticking clock** — a lot of forest becomes ready to harvest inside the
   same ~25-year window that new power tech matures. Nice timing, if we use it.
3. **No pull to replant** — cutting sugi for cheap lumber doesn't pay enough to
   fund putting anything back. Short-term profit, repeat forever, nobody's
   incentivized to fix the cycle.

## 5. The plan — think sideways (~10 min)

1. **Pull, don't push.** An AI data center + biomass CHP plant creates a buyer
   for wood that actually pays — that pulls sustainable forestry forward instead
   of everyone waiting for a subsidy.
2. **The trade:** clear the sugi monoculture, put native broadleaf back —
   good for wildlife, good for the watershed, and it's the "25 years to grow the
   village" half of the slogan.
3. **Proof it's not just us dreaming** — Tenkawa's already doing a version of
   this next door (photos). Borrow the parts that work.
4. **A/B/C/D wood, not just A.** Right now only top-grade A wood is worth cutting.
   CHP fuel + a data-center customer means B/C/D grade (branches, crooked logs,
   the stuff nobody wants) suddenly has a buyer too.
5. **The landowner payout.** Electricity + compute revenue means we can offer
   landowners *more* than today's crashed lumber price — the missing incentive
   that gets the 25-year replant cycle actually moving.
