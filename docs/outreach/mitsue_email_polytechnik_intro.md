## Draft Email — English version (primary)

**To:** office@polytechnik.com
**Subject:** Small-scale biomass CHP for electricity generation + AI data center — Mitsue Village, Nara, Japan

---

Dear Mr. Schirnhofer,

My name is Rob Oudendijk. I have lived in Japan for 35 years, the last 15 of them in the forest of Mitsue Village (御杖村), Nara Prefecture, in a self-designed, self-built off-grid house. I work on a small rural-revitalization project here, combining forest restoration, biomass energy, and small-scale AI compute infrastructure.

Mitsue has large areas of aging, under-managed sugi (cedar) plantation, and — like much of rural Japan — a shortage of forestry workers rather than a shortage of land. Thinning alone doesn't generate enough revenue to sustainably support forestry work. Our proposed model is to clear-fell the aging sugi at scale and use the harvested wood to fuel a biomass CHP (combined heat and power) plant, where the electricity is the primary product: sold to the grid and/or used directly to power a small AI data center built alongside the plant. Energy and compute revenue funds the ongoing forestry work, turning forest restoration into a self-financing activity rather than a subsidy-dependent one.

I read an interview in which you mentioned Polytechnik has been active in Japan since 2004 — that practical, long-running Japan experience (including heat/cogeneration installations at schools and spas) is exactly the kind of track record we're looking for in a technology partner, even though our own priority is electricity output rather than heat.

At this early stage, we are not yet in a position to purchase or commit to a project — we are gathering technical information to understand what is realistic at this scale. Could you share:

- The smallest plant sizes in your current cogeneration lineup, specifically their electrical output (kWe) and electrical efficiency, not just heat capacity
- Typical wood chip fuel specifications (moisture content, chip size) your systems require
- Whether grid-sale of electricity is a standard configuration for your Japan installations, and whether any are visitable

I understand, as you said, that Japan requires time to build the right relationship — we would welcome the chance to start that conversation now, even at this early and informal stage.

Thank you very much for your time.

Rob Oudendijk
