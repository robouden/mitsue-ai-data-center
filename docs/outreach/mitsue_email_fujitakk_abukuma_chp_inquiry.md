## Notes
- Confirmed technical facts already in hand (from public sources, see below) — don't re-ask:
  E3 = 25 kWel (own use), WE-50P = 49.9 kWel (FIT), combined 180 kWth heat output; measured
  ratio ~1 kWh elec + 2 kWth heat per 1 kg pellet; new building completed Nov 2018; partners
  = Fukushima Prefecture, Entrade Energiesysteme GmbH (Germany, NRW), Fukushima University,
  Energy Agency Fukushima, Fujita Construction Industry.
- Gaps this inquiry targets: capex, annual fuel tonnage/cost, and real operational
  history/maintenance — none of which are published.
- Entrade is an **imported (German) maker** — per [[project_dc_network_product_requirement]]-
  adjacent policy in `mitsue_chp_maker_shortlist.md`, imported units are benchmark-only for
  Mitsue's commercial tier, but this is exactly the kind of real operating data we want for
  Tier A prototype benchmarking regardless of maker nationality.

**Note for when they reply — pellets vs. chips, don't apply the ratio directly:**
The measured "1 kg pellet → 1 kWh elec + 2 kWth" figure is a **pellet-fuel** ratio (pellets:
~8-10% moisture, ~4.6-4.8 kWh/kg calorific value, ENplus A1 spec). Mitsue's planned fuel is
**wood chips**, not pellets — chips at ~20% moisture after drying carry meaningfully less
energy per kg (~3.0-3.5 kWh/kg vs. ~4.7 kWh/kg for pellets). At the same CHP conversion
efficiency, a chip-fed system needs roughly **30-40% more fuel mass per kWh** than the pellet
figure implies. When their reply comes in, check: (a) is their unit pellet-only or can it run
raw/dried chips, and (b) recompute any tonnage figures they give using chip calorific value,
not the pellet ratio as-is.
