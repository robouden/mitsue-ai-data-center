A first version of this email was sent via their contact form on 2026-05-24 with no reply; this is a refreshed send reflecting the project's current state (advisors added, site options clarified) rather than a "checking in" follow-up.

## How to approach HIGHRESO

HIGHRESO is a close operating analogue to the Mitsue-kun project in Japan: they run air-cooled data centers in repurposed buildings (空き施設活用) and publish a PUE target of under 1.1. They are a company at meaningful scale — Mitsue is small, rural, and earlier-stage — so the contact should be **respectful, learning-oriented, and short**.

**How we found you:** While researching repurposed-facility data centers in Japan, we identified HIGHRESO as the closest operating analogue to what Mitsue is attempting. Since the May send got no response and enough has changed on the project side to be worth a fresh attempt, this is written as a new first-touch-style email rather than referencing the earlier one — the earlier draft was never acknowledged, so nothing is gained by pointing back to it.

Three rules for the email:

1. **Japanese first.** This is a Japanese company being approached about a Japanese rural project. Japanese-primary signals respect and competence; an English-only first email is a common mis-step.
2. **No partnership ask yet.** The first email asks for a short introductory conversation only — not a memorandum of understanding, not investment, not a site visit. Save those for later contacts once mutual interest is established.
3. **Show you've done your homework.** Reference one specific thing from their site (the PUE <1.1 figure, the repurposed-facility model, or the Ayagawa Kagawa facility). This signals that the email is informed and not generic outreach.

### How to find the right contact

- Start with their **website contact form** at https://highreso.jp/contact — this is the official, low-friction route and is normal for first-touch in Japan
- If you can identify a named contact (via their site, press coverage, or a mutual connection), a direct email is **stronger** than the form — but the form is a perfectly acceptable first-touch route
- Avoid going directly to executives via LinkedIn InMail for first contact; the contact form is the culturally expected path

### What to expect

- A reply may take **1–4 weeks** — Japanese B2B response timing is slower than Western norms; do not follow up before two weeks
- The first reply, if positive, will likely be a brief acknowledgement and a request to schedule a short call (30 min)
- Do not attach the full project documentation to the first email. If they ask for more, send the **English README + Japanese README** and the **introduction A4** — nothing heavier

### After the first contact

If they engage, consider — *in this order*:
1. A 30-minute video call to introduce the project and learn from their experience
2. A site visit to one of their facilities (Ayagawa or similar) — only after the call
3. Discussion of any concrete collaboration (technical advisory, knowledge sharing, possibly a referral to their engineering partners)

A formal partnership ask should only come after points 1–3 have established mutual interest.
