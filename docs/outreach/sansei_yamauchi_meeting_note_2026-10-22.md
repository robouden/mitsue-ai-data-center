**杉本様のご意見(要点)**
1. **単一樹種**:森林の多様性の面から、桐だけを植えるのは賛成できない。
2. **獣害・土壌**:天川村では、鹿のいる伐採跡地にも桐が天然に生えている。撹乱された土と日当たりを好み、成長は早い。土壌への悪影響はないと思う。ただし、全部が大木になるかは未確認で、5年で収穫できるかは分からない。
3. **混植**:他の広葉樹やスギとの混植は有りだと思う。
4. **奈良での生育と買取先**:標高1,000m付近でも天然の桐はよく入るが、いつか消えて大木は少ない。標高300m程度の低山では、日当たりのよい場所で大木も見かける。奈良で桐の収穫を目的に植林している人はおらず、近畿には買取先がないと思う。
5. **バイオマス**:桐の木部は密度が低く(軽く)、エネルギーが小さいため、燃料には不向きだと思う。
6. **野生動物と桐の寿命(10月15日夜の追加回答)**:天川村はシカやクマの生息地だが、桐は獣害で消えるというより、環境の変化(適正な生育環境の喪失)で寿命が短くなるのではないか。獣害については、桐は「好んで食べられる」樹種ではないと思う。「女の子が生まれたら庭に桐を植えなさい」という言い伝えがあり、嫁入りの頃に衣装ダンス(吸湿性が良い)を作るためで、庭のように人が管理し、日当たりが良く競合する木がない環境で、20年ほどで大きく育つ。自然の中でそのような環境を保つのは難しく、桐は裸地にいち早く入るパイオニアだが、後から入る耐陰性のある樹種に自然に淘汰されていくのではないか。これは日々の実感で、学術的な裏付けはない。**私の意見として山内様にお伝えいただいて問題ない**とのこと。

## English

Dear Yamauchi-san,

Ahead of our meeting on 22 October, here is feedback from a local expert on planting kiri. It is for reference only. It is not our policy, and the decision on your land is yours.

**Source:** Sugimoto Kazuya (Sugimoto Forest Management Office, Tenkawa, Nara), a licensed forest manager (森林総合監理士). He replied on 15 October, as his personal view.

**His views, in short**
1. **Monoculture:** He does not favour planting kiri alone, for the sake of forest diversity.
2. **Deer, soil:** In Tenkawa kiri comes up naturally even on deer-affected clear-cuts. It likes disturbed soil and sun, and grows fast. He thinks soil impact is nil. But he has not confirmed that all trees become large, and he doesn't know if 5-year harvest is possible.
3. **Mixing:** Mixing with other broadleaves or sugi is fine.
4. **Growth in Nara, buyers:** Natural kiri is common even near 1,000 m but tends to disappear; big trees are rare. At ~300 m in sunny spots he sees large single trees. Nobody in Nara plants kiri to harvest it, and he thinks Kinki has no buyer.
5. **Biomass:** Kiri wood is very low in density, so low in energy, and he considers it unsuitable as fuel.
6. **Wildlife and kiri lifespan (follow-up reply, 15 Oct evening):** Tenkawa is deer and bear habitat, but he thinks kiri is not so much lost to animal damage as short-lived because the growing conditions change (it loses its suitable habitat). On damage: it is not a species animals "like to eat". There is a saying, "when a girl is born, plant a kiri in the garden": by the time she marries, the wood makes a clothes chest (good humidity control). Kiri grows large in ~20 years in a garden because people manage it, it gets full sun and has no rival trees. Keeping such conditions in nature is hard. Kiri is a pioneer that enters bare ground fast, but shade-tolerant species arriving later naturally outcompete it. This is his everyday impression, not academic evidence. **He is fine with us telling Yamauchi-san these are his views.**

**Our research (for reference)**
- 20 cm trunk diameter at 5 years has been reported on managed, favourable sites.
- For sawlogs, fast growth means 10–15 years; the tight-grained wood the Japanese market prefers takes 20–30 years.
- Japanese kiri demand is about one-eighth of its peak and imports have displaced domestic wood.

**Questions for the meeting**
- Who buys at year 5? Do you know a buyer?
- How will you protect against deer, bear and frost?
- Sugimoto-san says kiri is a pioneer that later shade-tolerant trees outcompete. Does that match what you see on your land?
- Would you consider kiri on part of the land with broadleaves on the rest?
- What is the current state of the Ureshino (Mie) forest? Has it been cut, what is standing there, and when will kiri be planted?
- For 26 October: how many trees, what area, which seedlings?

**About the flowers (added)**
- **Kiri flowers are edible.** They taste and smell sweet, like vanilla and honey. Fresh flowers could be a new product and a good start for a new business.
- **A question:** Do you plan to spray chemicals (pesticides or herbicides) on the trees or flowers? Edible use requires no spraying.
- **A planting idea:** On your mountain, the south-facing side could be kiri, and the north and other sides a mixed forest (broadleaves and others).
- **The name:** The scientific name *Paulownia* honours Anna Pavlovna (1795–1865), Queen of the Netherlands and daughter of Tsar Paul I of Russia. As a Dutchman, I like that connection. (Wikipedia: Anna Pavlovna of Russia.)

**Our position:** In Mitsue the base approach is native broadleaf that wildlife can feed on.
