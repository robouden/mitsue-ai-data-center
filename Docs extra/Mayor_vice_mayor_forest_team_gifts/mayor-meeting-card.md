# Mayor & Vice Mayor — Kaya Seed Presentation Card

**Occasion:** Presentation of hand-collected kaya (榧) seeds in a self-made
sugi (cedar) box with kintsugi gold-flake seam.

**Three voices, three timescales — keep them visually separated:**

| Location              | Voice                          | Timescale / Meaning      |
|-----------------------|--------------------------------|--------------------------|
| Outside of card / lid | Tanka (gold)                   | Future (100 years)       |
| Inside lid (smaller)  | Kintsugi line                  | Present (the project)    |
| Beside the seeds      | Scent haiku + skin remedy      | Now (this moment)        |
| Bottom of the box     | Cedar sawdust from local mill  | Community (village hands)|

---

## 1. Tanka — outside of card / box lid

> 榧の実を　手づから集め　御杖村にて
>
> 百年先の　木陰を植える

*Kaya no mi wo / tezukara atsume / Mitsue nite /*
*hyakunen-saki no / kokage wo ueru*

> Kaya seeds, gathered by my own hand, here in Mitsue —
> planting the shade of a hundred years from now.

---

## 2. Kintsugi line — inside lid (smaller, in gold)

> 金継ぎのごとく — 割れ目を金にて継ぎ、村を強くする

*Kintsugi no gotoku — wareme wo kin nite tsugi, mura wo tsuyoku suru*

> Like kintsugi — joining the cracks with gold,
> making the village stronger.

**English line (other side of card):**

> Like kintsugi, we mend what was broken with gold —
> and what is mended is stronger than what was whole.

---

## 3. Scent haiku & skin remedy — beside the seeds (smallest)

> 掌に　榧の香ひそむ　恵みかな

*Tenohira ni / kaya no ka hisomu / megumi kana*

> In the palm —
> kaya's quiet scent hidden,
> a small blessing.

**English note (for inside the lid, near the seeds):**

> Rub a seed gently between your palms —
> the warm oil softens the skin and leaves its scent for hours.
> Kaya oil has been a quiet remedy in Japan since ancient times:
> the seed is both gift and small medicine.

**Japanese companion line (alongside, in smaller script):**

> 古より、榧の油は肌をやさしく潤し、香りを長く残すと伝えられる。

*Inishie yori, kaya no abura wa hada wo yasashiku uruoshi, kaori wo nagaku nokosu to tsutaerareru.*

> Since ancient times, kaya oil has been said to gently soften the skin
> and let its fragrance linger.

## Variant — for the Forestry Team

Same three verses, but **swap the front-face dedication**:

> 杉の箱に　榧の実を入れて　森の手へ
>
> 切るは今日　植えるは百年　共にゆく

*Sugi no hako ni / kaya no mi wo irete / mori no te e /*
*kiru wa kyō / ueru wa hyakunen / tomo ni yuku*

> Into a cedar box, kaya seeds — for the hands of the forest.
> Today we cut; for a hundred years we plant; we go together.

The cedar box itself carries the meaning more directly for the forestry team —
the wood is *their* wood, the seam is *their* repair, and the seeds are *their*
next century of work. Their box's kintsugi seam can be larger and more visible
than the mayor's — they'll appreciate the honesty.

## Material & Making Notes

**Box (sugi / cedar, self-made):**
- Saw or split a single sugi panel, rejoin with clear two-part epoxy + real
  gold flakes (kinpun 金粉) suspended in the resin.
- Sand the seam flush, but leave it *very slightly* proud — real kintsugi
  sits raised, not flat.
- Leave the cedar unfinished or rub with camellia oil (椿油). The cedar's
  scent will mingle with the kaya's when the box is opened.

**Bed of cedar sawdust (bottom of the box):**
- Sawdust from a local Mitsue mill — from the owner Rob knows personally.
- Lay a soft bed of fresh cedar sawdust in the bottom of the box; rest
  the three kaya seeds on top.
- The cedar sawdust scent will mingle with the kaya's the moment the
  lid is lifted — a layered, immediate sensory greeting.
- **Symbolic layer:** the new growth (kaya seeds) rests on the bed of
  what is being cut (cedar). And the bed itself is the gift of a
  community member — so the village is literally inside the box, under
  the seeds. The community is not described in the card; it is felt
  when the lid opens.
- Use *fresh* sawdust (collected near the time of the meeting) — older
  sawdust loses its scent quickly.

**Card:**
- Gold ink (or gold foil hot-stamp) on deep indigo or sumi-black washi.
- If handwritten: fude pen with gold ink. Imperfect is better than printed.
- Three seeds per recipient — echoes 三 in 三津江.

**Kaya oil — folk use to remember when presenting:**
- When a kaya seed is rubbed between warm palms, a small amount of oil
  is released along with the fragrance.
- That oil has been used in Japan since ancient times as a gentle skin
  remedy — for dryness, irritation, and as a mild fragrance.
- Mention this quietly when handing over the seeds, especially if the
  vice mayor takes one in hand. The gift then carries an immediate,
  physical kindness alongside the symbolic one.

**Spoken framing (only if it lands naturally):**
> "The trees we cut for this project are cedar.
>  The trees we plant back are kaya — slower, more valuable, more patient.
>  That is the trade I want to make with Mitsue."

> "Rub one between your palms — the oil is gentle on the skin,
>  and the scent will stay with you for the rest of the day."

> "The bed under the seeds is cedar sawdust from [mill owner's name]'s
>  mill, here in Mitsue. Even the ground the seeds rest on comes from
>  the hands of this village."
