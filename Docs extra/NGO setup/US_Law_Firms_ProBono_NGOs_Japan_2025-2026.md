## How to Get Involved with These Firms

### For NGO/ISs Seeking Pro Bono Legal Services:

1. **Contact the firm's Pro Bono Department** - Most have dedicated teams
2. **Identify specific legal needs** - Match your ISH's requirements with their expertise areas
3. **Submit applications** - Typically requires documentation of mission, financial status, and legal needs
4. **Attend firm-hosted events** - Pro bono roundtables and information sessions
5. **Engage with LEDGE** - Acts as intermediary between US firms and Japanese NGOs

### For Law Firm Employees Wanting to Volunteer:

1. **Internal volunteering portals** - Most firms have employee resource sites
2. **Pro Bono Committee participation** - Internal application processes
3 **CSR/Community service days** - Often organized annually
4. **Skill-based volunteering** - Match professional expertise with NGO needs

## How to Start a Partnership

### For ISHs:

1. **Prepare Documentation**:
   - Mission statement and vision
   - Financial needs and constraints
   - Specific legal requirements
   - Timeline for services needed

2. **Identify Priority Areas**:
   - Corporate registration and compliance
   - Tax-exempt status applications
   - Contract drafting and review
   - Intellectual property protection
   - Labor and employment law compliance
   - International partnerships and funding agreements

3. **Research Firm Alignments**:
   - Match your ISH's mission with firm's CSR focus areas
   - Consider geographic proximity (Nara area vs. Tokyo)
   - Evaluate firm's experience with Japanese NGOs vs. international NGOs

4. **Reach Out Strategically**:
   - Contact the firm's CSR/Pro Bono department directly
   - Reference specific firm initiatives that align with your ISH's work
   - Provide clear, concise information about your needs
   - Be patient - most firms review requests quarterly

### For Law Firms:

1. **Establish Clear Processes**:
   - Define pro bono eligibility criteria
   - Create case assignment protocols
   - Develop documentation and reporting systems
   - Set clear expectations and timelines

2. **Build Local Networks**:
   - Partner with Japanese bar associations
   - Collaborate with established NGOs like JAR
   - Join platforms like LEDGE for case matching

3. **Measure Impact**:
   - Track hours donated and cases won/lost
   - Document beneficiary feedback and outcomes
   - Report on CSR/ESG metrics

## Conclusion

US law firms in Japan have significantly expanded their pro bono and CSR programs supporting NGOs and ISHs. The trend is moving from individual partner initiatives to firm-wide, structured programs that are increasingly integrated with corporate ESG goals.

For ISHs seeking pro bono legal services, the window of opportunity is favorable, with firms actively seeking partnerships and establishing dedicated processes to match legal expertise with NGO needs.

Key recommendations for ISHs:
1. Start with firms that have demonstrated interest in social justice and human rights
2. Consider both Tokyo-based firms (more resources) and regional firms (potentially more available)
3. Be prepared to document your needs and demonstrate alignment with firm values
4. Consider engaging with intermediary organizations like LEDGE to streamline the process

The collaboration between US law firms and Japanese NGOs represents a growing and impactful trend that continues to expand and evolve as both legal and social landscapes change.
