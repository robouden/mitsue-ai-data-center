I see the forest that the older people planted becoming the power for their grandchildren. In the same way, the hinoki from Aomi at Sugano Organic — planted by his grandfather — became, through the help of Niwa-san, the beautiful desk in this office.

I want the older people to feel that the trees they sowed will become the power that stores their memories, and the memories of the whole world, in an AI data center. Their knowledge and wisdom power the newest generation.

But we need to provide the "glue": the commercial structure that makes this happen — the power that educates their children. A local AI data center in the school puts the education of their grandchildren back in the same place where they themselves were taught.

I want the younger people to see what their grandparents made for them, and to appreciate the foresight they had. It was not exactly the same idea they had when they sowed the seeds.

I want that same kaya tree, where the older people used to collect kaya for their snacks, to become the power that returns.

I have been observing this village for many years now, and I think the time is right to put all the parts together.

I am starting to build a team — an organisation of experts from all over the world in technology, finance, and human resources. Educational and economic experts are advising me on how to build a resilient structure for the decades to come.

My hope for the village is that the billiard cues are made from the same hinoki as the Sugano Organic desk, and the billiard tables are built by Niwa from wood from the nearby forest.

I hope the new billiard venue will help educate the children — not like the earlier BlueRose events did with their venues — and that it will be powered by local finance, not by shady construction companies.

I want the old wooden school be be used a what it was intended to serve the comunity. To educate the younger generation for their future. To give them the knowledge and the tools to flourish.
