I see the forest that the older generation planted becoming the power for their grandchildren. It is the same story as the hinoki from Tokuo Aomi at Sugano Organic: a tree planted by his grandfather that became, through the hands of Niwa-san, the beautiful desk in this office.

I want the older people to feel that the trees they once sowed will become the power that stores their memories — and the memories of the whole world — inside an AI data center. Their knowledge and wisdom will drive the newest generation.

But for this to happen, we need to provide the glue: the commercial structure that ties it all together, and the power that educates their children. A local AI data center placed in the school returns the education of their grandchildren to the very place where they themselves were once taught.

I want the younger people to see what their grandparents made for them, and to appreciate the foresight behind it. When the older generation sowed those seeds, this was not exactly the future they had in mind — and that makes it all the more meaningful.

I want that same kaya tree, where the older people once gathered kaya for their snacks, to become the power that gives back.

I have observed this village for many years, and I believe the time is finally right to bring all the parts together.

I am beginning to build a team: an organisation of experts from around the world in technology, finance, and human resources. Educators and economists are advising me on how to build a structure resilient enough to last for decades.

My hope for the village is that the billiard cues will be made from the same hinoki as the Sugano Organic desk, and that the billiard tables will be built by Niwa from wood taken from the nearby forest.

I want the old wooden school be be used a what it was intended to serve the comunity. To educate the younger generation for their future. To give them the knowledge and the tools to flourish.
