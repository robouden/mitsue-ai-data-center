# Mitsue Village Forestry Cooperative (御杖村森林組合)

> 先人が植えた森が、今、村に豊かさをもたらす。

Here's what I was able to piece together about 御杖村森林組合 (Mitsue Village Forestry Cooperative). A fair amount is well-documented; the financials are the gap, for reasons I'll explain.

### Basics

It's a forestry cooperative (森林組合) headquartered at 奈良県宇陀郡御杖村大字菅野1581番地, the same Sugano area where you are. Its corporate number is 2150005004376, registered as an "other incorporated-by-registration legal entity" (the standard legal form for a 森林組合 under the Forestry Cooperative Act). Phone is 0745-95-2010, hours 8:30–17:00, closed weekends and holidays. The official site is mitsuemori.org (somewhat dated/under construction in places). [gBizINFO](https://info.gbiz.go.jp/hojin/ichiran?hojinBango=2150005004376)[Jalan](https://www.jalan.net/kankou/spt_29386ga3550157067/)

It was founded in January 1952 (昭和27年1月) — so over 70 years old, dating to the early postwar afforestation era when sugi/hinoki plantation forestry was being established across rural Japan. [Wixsite](https://narawoodjob.wixsite.com/narawoodjob/blank-6)

### How it's structured and run

As a 森林組合 it's a member-owned cooperative governed under the Forestry Cooperative Act, meaning forest-owning members (組合員) hold shares (出資金), elect a board of directors (理事会) led by a representative director / cooperative head (代表理事組合長), with a general meeting of members as the top decision body. It belongs to the prefectural federation, the 奈良県森林組合連合会 (Nara Forestry Cooperative Federation), and is a certified legal-timber supplier (合法木材供給事業者) under that federation's scheme.

I couldn't retrieve the current 組合長's name — the public corporate registry (gBizINFO) and the legal-timber certification page both leave the representative field blank, and the cooperative doesn't publish a board roster online. That's something you'd likely get faster through the village office or a direct call given your proximity.

Internally it's organized into three departments: a guidance/instruction division (指導部門), a forest-maintenance division (森林整備部門), and a wood-processing division (木材加工部門). [Wixsite](https://narawoodjob.wixsite.com/narawoodjob/blank-6)

### Operations

The three divisions break down as follows:

Forest maintenance (森林整備課) does surveying, tree selection, felling, log-making, extraction/hauling, forest surveys, work-road maintenance, sales of forestry materials, and machine repair. This is the field-work core. [Wixsite](https://narawoodjob.wixsite.com/narawoodjob/blank-6)

The guidance division handles training of forestry successors, promoting stable supply of prefecture-grown timber, issuing various certificates, promoting timber extraction, running workshops, member communications, consultation on forest maintenance and silviculture, and forest-education support. [Wixsite](https://narawoodjob.wixsite.com/narawoodjob/blank-6)

The wood-processing division focuses on receiving firewood logs, firewood production, timber hauling, and sales of wood products. The cooperative also runs the 御杖村加工センター (Mitsue processing center), making products from thinned timber (間伐材). [Wixsite](https://narawoodjob.wixsite.com/narawoodjob/blank-6)

There's an administrative division too (総務課) handling invoicing, accounting, member management, and document work across the other divisions.

Historically it has pushed direct supply of Mitsue sugi/hinoki to homebuilders. Back in 2011 it launched a project to supply structural and floor/wall material directly to custom homebuilders in Mie and the Kinki region, framed around the idea that more use of planned thinnings supports forest protection and disaster prevention — and it noted a project adopted under a Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism scheme.

### People

This is a very small operation. The basic company profile lists 4 employees, and a recruiting interview gives more granular numbers: 3 regular staff with an average age of around 33 — an unusually young all-junior team for a forestry cooperative. A named snapshot from that interview (titles/tenure at time of writing): [Wixsite](https://narawoodjob.wixsite.com/narawoodjob/blank-6)[Wixsite](https://narawoodjob.wixsite.com/narawoodjob/blank-6)

- General Affairs Section Chief / accounting head — Kinsho (近處), ~15 years [Wixsite](https://narawoodjob.wixsite.com/narawoodjob/blank-6)
- Forest Maintenance Section Chief — Fukuzaki (福崎), ~7 years [Wixsite](https://narawoodjob.wixsite.com/narawoodjob/blank-6)
- Forest Maintenance — Tsuru (鶴), ~5 months [Wixsite](https://narawoodjob.wixsite.com/narawoodjob/blank-6)
- General Affairs — Takita (瀧田), ~1 year (temporary staff) [Wixsite](https://narawoodjob.wixsite.com/narawoodjob/blank-6)

They emphasize a strict, safety-first culture, with a claimed 8 consecutive years of zero workplace accidents and daily hiyari-hatto (near-miss) reporting. Benefits are notably generous for the sector: dependent/housing/commuting/travel allowances, uniforms and protective gear, annual full health checkups, staff housing in the village, relocation grants, and various congratulatory/condolence payments. [Wixsite](https://narawoodjob.wixsite.com/narawoodjob/blank-6)[Wixsite](https://narawoodjob.wixsite.com/narawoodjob/blank-6)

### Budget / financials

This is the genuine blind spot, and it's structural rather than just me missing it. Individual Japanese forestry cooperatives don't publicly disclose per-organization financials. The government corporate database (gBizINFO) shows the registration but leaves capital, employee count, revenue, and decisions blank — because a 森林組合 of this size files no securities reports (no EDINET entry) and its 決算 isn't published in 官報 in a way that's indexed openly. [gBizINFO](https://info.gbiz.go.jp/hojin/ichiran?hojinBango=2150005004376)

Where the numbers actually live: every forestry cooperative files an annual 業務報告書 / 決算関係書類 (covering 出資金, 事業別取扱高, balance sheet, P&L by business segment) to the Nara prefectural government and the federation, and the aggregate appears anonymized in the national 森林組合一斉調査 statistics on e-Stat. To get Mitsue's specific出資金, member count, managed forest area, and segment revenues, the realistic routes are: a direct request to the cooperative, the village office (御杖村役場 産業建設課 or similar), or the 奈良県森林組合連合会. Given a 3–4 person operation in a village of roughly 1,500 people, you're almost certainly looking at annual business turnover in the low tens of millions of yen at most, but I won't put a fabricated figure on it.

# 

Mitsue Village Forest Cooperative is a small forestry cooperative serving Mitsue Village in Nara Prefecture, Japan. It appears to be run as a local member-based cooperative focused on forest management and timber operations, with a very small staff and a practical, field-oriented structure.

## What it does

Its main activities include thinning, afforestation, timber production, forest surveying, road and work-trail maintenance, and the sale and maintenance of forestry machinery and materials. Publicly available job information also shows that it handles forest work and general administration side by side.

## How it is run

The cooperative is led by a representative director/chairperson and appears to operate with a simple division of labor between field staff and office staff. The available information suggests a compact management style rather than a large corporate hierarchy.

## Size

A recruitment page lists the organization as having 2 field workers and 2 office staff, so it is quite small. The same source also indicates a relatively young workforce, with field staff averaging in their late 20s.

## Financial scale

I could confirm its capital contribution amount, which is 31,346,000 yen. I could not confirm a full annual budget from the public sources I checked, so I cannot give a reliable budget figure.

## Work volume

Publicly listed recent activity includes about 65.98 hectares of thinning and 123 cubic meters of timber production in the most recent year shown. The year before that, the cooperative reported 68.3 hectares of thinning and 301 cubic meters of timber production.

## Other details

It is based at 1581 Sugano, Mitsue Village, Nara, and is also listed as a certified legal timber supplier. The village’s own materials mention that the cooperative also carries out its own forestry training and hands-on learning activities.

## Overall picture

In short, this is a very small but active local forestry cooperative that combines forest management, timber work, equipment-related services, and community-linked forestry activity. Its scale is modest, but its role in managing village forests appears substantial.
